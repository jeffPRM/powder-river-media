Social Media Marketing for Real Estate Agents: A Practical Guide

November 19, 20265 min read

Social media gives real estate agents an opportunity to do two things at once: market the properties they're selling and stay visible to people who may need an agent in the future.

The challenge is finding time to do it consistently.

Between showings, appointments, negotiations and paperwork, creating fresh content can easily fall to the bottom of the list. Fortunately, effective social media marketing doesn't require posting constantly or turning yourself into a full-time content creator.

A manageable strategy starts with understanding what your audience wants to see and making better use of the content you already have.

Choose the Platforms That Fit Your Business

You don't need to maintain an active presence on every social network.

Facebook and Instagram are natural starting points for many agents. Facebook is useful for sharing listings, local information and updates with an established network. Instagram emphasizes visual content, making it a good fit for property photography, short videos and behind-the-scenes posts.

YouTube provides another home for longer property videos, neighborhood guides and educational content. LinkedIn may be useful for agents focused on professional relationships, relocation referrals or commercial real estate.

Start with the platforms where you're most likely to reach your clients and referral network. A well-maintained presence on two platforms is more useful than five accounts that rarely get updated.

Share More Than Just Listings

Your listings should be an important part of your social media presence, but they shouldn't be the only thing people see.

Someone who isn't currently buying or selling might scroll past another property announcement. A useful explanation of the local market or a neighborhood recommendation may be more relevant to them.

Try rotating among a few types of content:

  • Listings: New properties, open houses, standout features and recent sales.
  • Local knowledge: Neighborhood highlights, community events and local amenities.
  • Educational posts: Answers to common questions about buying, selling and preparing a home for the market.
  • Your business: Client testimonials shared with permission, professional milestones and glimpses of your work.

This variety helps people get to know both your expertise and your personality.

Make Better Use of Your Listing Photography

Every professional listing shoot creates marketing material that can be used well beyond the MLS.

A strong exterior photograph can introduce a new listing. Interior photos can highlight a renovated kitchen, a spacious living area or an interesting architectural detail. Drone photographs can show a home's setting and surroundings.

Instead of posting every photo at once, consider using a few images in a carousel that tells a story about the property. Lead with a compelling photograph, then show the features most likely to interest your audience.

Professional real estate photography gives you a consistent visual foundation for these posts.

Be sure that listing details and availability remain accurate, especially when older posts continue circulating.

Use Video to Reach People Who Prefer Watching

Video adds movement and personality to your marketing.

A professionally produced vertical listing Reel can quickly introduce a home through a series of its most appealing spaces. It's designed for short-form platforms such as Instagram and Facebook Reels.

A longer, horizontal cinematic property walkthrough serves a different purpose. It gives viewers more time to explore the property and works well on YouTube and property websites.

Agents can also create casual videos on their phones. A quick neighborhood introduction, an explanation of a frequently asked real estate question or a personal update doesn't require a professional production.

Every video with spoken dialogue should have captions or subtitles. Many people scroll through social media with their sound turned off, and captions allow them to follow what you're saying without turning up the volume. They also make your videos more accessible to viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing.

The two approaches complement each other. Professional listing media presents the property, while everyday agent-created content helps viewers become familiar with the person behind the business.

Turn One Listing into Several Posts

A single listing can provide material for multiple posts without repeating the same announcement.

For example, an agent could share:

  1. A new-listing post featuring the strongest exterior photograph.
  2. A short Reel introducing the property.
  3. A photo carousel highlighting three notable features.
  4. An open house announcement.
  5. A follow-up post after the property sells, with the seller's permission.

These posts serve different purposes throughout the listing's marketing cycle.

A property with an interesting location might also support a neighborhood post. A home with distinctive outdoor features could provide a useful opportunity to explain how buyers evaluate outdoor living space.

The goal is to get more value from the work already being done, not manufacture content for the sake of filling a calendar.

Build a Schedule You Can Actually Maintain

Consistency matters more than an ambitious schedule that lasts two weeks.

For a busy agent, a simple weekly plan might include one listing-related post, one educational or neighborhood post and one short video or personal update.

That's a starting point, not a required posting frequency.

You can also prepare content in batches. When you're reviewing a listing's photographs, select a few images for future posts. When you're recording a short video, consider whether a portion of it could be useful as a separate clip.

Planning ahead makes it easier to stay visible during busy weeks.

Give People a Reason to Contact You

Social media engagement is encouraging, but likes and views aren't necessarily the best measures of business success.

Consider whether your posts lead to meaningful conversations, property inquiries, website visits, referrals or appointments.

Make it easy for someone to take the next step. Your profile should clearly identify the area you serve and provide a convenient way to contact you.

Individual posts can also include a natural invitation to ask a question, arrange a showing or learn more about a property.

You don't need an aggressive sales pitch in every caption. Useful content and approachable communication can do much of that work.

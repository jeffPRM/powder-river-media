When Should Real Estate Agents Use Drone Photography?

November 19, 20265 min read

Professional real estate photography shows buyers what a home looks like. Drone photography adds another perspective, showing how the house sits on its property and how that property fits into its surroundings.

For agents, that can make a big difference in how a listing is presented.

Aerial images can reveal the size of a backyard, the relationship between a house and its outbuildings or the proximity of a property to nearby amenities. They can also provide an attractive elevated view of a home that simply isn't possible from the sidewalk.

Here are some of the situations where drone photography is particularly useful for real estate listings.

1. Properties with Large Lots or Acreage

A five-acre property is difficult to appreciate through ground-level photography alone.

Photographs of the house, yard and surrounding landscape may all look appealing, but buyers still have to piece together how everything fits.

Drone photos provide that context. A wider aerial view can show the property's setting, open areas, wooded sections and the relationship between buildings.

This is especially useful for properties in areas such as Monkton, Sparks, Glen Arm and northern Baltimore County, where larger lots and rural settings are common.

For properties with substantial acreage, aerial photography helps buyers understand what they're looking at before they visit.

2. Homes with Interesting Outdoor Features

Pools, patios, decks, gardens, detached garages and outdoor entertaining areas can be major selling points.

The challenge is showing how these features relate to one another.

Imagine a property with a swimming pool, a covered patio and a landscaped backyard. Individual photographs can showcase each feature, but an aerial image reveals the overall arrangement.

Drone photography can be particularly helpful for homes with:

  • Swimming pools and outdoor entertaining spaces.
  • Large decks, patios and landscaped yards.
  • Detached garages, workshops and accessory buildings.
  • Long driveways or distinctive property layouts.
  • Extensive gardens and outdoor recreational areas.

These perspectives complement traditional listing photographs rather than replacing them.

3. Homes with Appealing Locations

Sometimes a property's location is just as important as the house itself.

A waterfront home near Annapolis, for example, may benefit from an aerial photograph showing its relationship to the water. A home near a golf course, park or wooded preserve might have surroundings that are difficult to communicate through ordinary exterior photographs.

Drone photography provides a wider view that helps buyers understand the setting.

This can also be valuable for suburban listings. A well-positioned aerial image might show a home's relationship to neighboring streets, open spaces or other notable features.

It's important, however, to distinguish visual proximity from actual access. A drone photograph showing a nearby park doesn't necessarily establish that the property has direct access to it.

4. Homes with Architectural Features That Are Difficult to Photograph

Some homes are challenging to capture from ground level.

A steep driveway, elevated foundation, unusual roofline or large front elevation may make it difficult to find a photograph that shows the entire building attractively.

An elevated drone photograph can solve that problem.

Drone photography doesn't always mean a dramatic bird's-eye view from hundreds of feet overhead. Sometimes a modestly elevated perspective is all that's needed to show the home's design and setting more effectively.

This approach can work particularly well for larger homes, distinctive architecture and properties where landscaping or terrain limits the available ground-level angles.

5. Luxury Real Estate Listings

Luxury listings often have more features to communicate, from outdoor living areas and elaborate landscaping to architectural details and extensive grounds.

Drone photography helps bring those elements together.

An aerial image can introduce the property, establish its setting and give buyers a sense of its overall scale before they explore the interior photographs.

Aerial footage can also become part of a cinematic real estate walkthrough, connecting the home's exterior setting with the spaces inside.

The goal isn't simply to make a listing look expensive. It's to help buyers appreciate the property as a whole.

What About Smaller Homes and Townhouses?

Drone photography isn't limited to luxury homes or rural estates.

A Baltimore rowhome, townhouse in Columbia or suburban home in Towson may also benefit from an elevated exterior view.

In a densely built neighborhood, a drone can offer a different perspective on the building, backyard or surrounding streets. It can also help establish where the property sits within the neighborhood.

The most useful aerial photograph isn't necessarily the highest or widest shot. It's the one that communicates something relevant about the property.

Drone Photography vs. Drone Video

Drone photos and drone video serve different purposes.

Drone photographs work well in MLS galleries, property websites and print marketing. Buyers can examine the images at their own pace, alongside the rest of the listing photographs.

Drone video adds movement. It can reveal the property gradually, move across the landscape or establish the setting at the beginning of a listing walkthrough.

For listings using both photography and video, these formats work together to create a more complete presentation.

Are There Restrictions on Real Estate Drone Photography?

Yes. Drone photography for real estate marketing is a commercial activity regulated by the Federal Aviation Administration.

Commercial drone pilots generally operate under the FAA's Part 107 rules, which include pilot certification and operational requirements.

Some locations also require airspace authorization. This is particularly relevant around airports, where permission may be needed before a drone can operate in controlled airspace.

The FAA provides systems such as LAANC for requesting certain airspace authorizations. Restrictions and safety considerations can affect whether a particular flight is permitted.

Agents should work with a qualified drone operator who understands these requirements rather than assuming every property can be photographed from the air.

For more information, see the FAA's guidance for commercial drone operators.

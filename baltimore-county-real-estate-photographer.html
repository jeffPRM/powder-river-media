A little history

Baltimore County was formed in 1659, long before the city of Baltimore existed, and Towson became its seat in 1854. The city has been separate from the county since 1851.

Where county residents commute

Many residents work in the county itself, in Towson, Hunt Valley, White Marsh and Owings Mills. Others commute to downtown Baltimore and Johns Hopkins, to the Social Security Administration in Woodlawn, to BWI Airport and to Washington. I-83, I-95, I-70 and the Beltway are the main roads, and the light rail serves Lutherville, Timonium, Cockeysville and Hunt Valley.

The job market

Baltimore County is the second largest job center in Maryland. The Social Security Administration and the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services are headquartered here, and T. Rowe Price, BD and McCormick are major employers. Towson University and the Greater Baltimore Medical Center add education and health care jobs. The Tradepoint Atlantic logistics campus has brought Amazon, BMW and Home Depot into the county, creating a growing warehouse and distribution sector.

Where the market has been hot, 2024 to 2026

Redfin's early 2026 ranking of the fastest-selling cities in Maryland put Timonium in the top ten. Carney, Arbutus, Lansdowne, Dundalk and Perry Hall also made the top thirty, and that list is based on how quickly listings went under contract at the end of 2025. Redfin also rated the 21204 ZIP code around Towson as very competitive in the fall of 2025.

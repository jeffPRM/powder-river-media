HDR listing photos for agents across Central Maryland

What listing photos need to do

Most buyers first see a home on a listing website or their phone. They scroll through the photos and decide whether it is worth a visit. Photos that are dark, crooked or hard to read give buyers a reason to keep scrolling. A listing photo has one job: letting a buyer understand the home without being in it. I wrote more about this in why professional photos matter in real estate.

That means each room is shot from a spot that shows its size and layout, the vertical lines are straight and the colors look the way they do in person. Windows matter too. A room can look fine while the window is a blown-out white rectangle, and that tells a buyer nothing about the light or the view. I do a window pull, balancing the exposure so the room and the window both stay readable.

How an HDR real estate photography shoot works

I shoot every property in HDR, which combines several exposures of the same frame so bright windows and darker corners both show up. After the shoot I process and color-correct every image, so the walls are the color of the walls and the wood looks like wood.

The files arrive sized for the MLS and without a logo on them, so they are ready to use wherever you market the listing. You can see the results in my portfolio.

Baltimore homes are not all alike

A narrow rowhome with exposed brick in Canton is a different job from a suburban colonial in Timonium, a condo with a view in Harbor East or a waterfront house with a dock in Severna Park. Rowhomes often have windows only at the front and back, so the middle of the house can run dark, and I pay attention to how the light travels through it. Brick and other rich colors can throw a color cast over a room, and I correct for it.

Condos often come with shared spaces, and I can photograph a lobby, fitness room or roof deck if you want them in the listing. Larger properties, corner lots and waterfront homes are good candidates for drone photography, since an aerial shot shows the lot and what is nearby.

What is included

Each shoot covers the interior and exterior of the home, processed and color-corrected. The number of photos depends on the property, but every room is covered, inside and out. Agents often ask for these add-ons:

A twilight edit turns a daytime exterior into a warm, evening-light version, which suits a front-of-house hero image. Virtual staging furnishes vacant rooms digitally, so buyers can picture the space. Amenity photos cover community spaces such as a pool, fitness center or clubhouse. I list pricing on request, so ask when you book.

Add more than photos

Photos do the most work when buyers can also see the layout. A floor plan shows how the rooms connect, with room measurements, so buyers can read the layout before they visit. A video tour shows how one space flows into the next, and a 3D tour lets buyers walk through at their own pace. You can book them with the photography or on their own, and you can see everything in one place on the services page.

Questions agents ask about listing photography

Do I own the photos?

You have broad usage rights. The photos are delivered without branding, so you can use them on the MLS, on your website, in your brokerage's marketing and on social media.

Are the photos ready for the MLS?

Yes. They arrive processed, color-corrected and sized to MLS specifications, ready to upload.

How soon will I have the photos?

Photos are delivered within 24 hours of the shoot. Some of my other services take longer.

Do I need to be at the shoot?

Not necessarily. You can send me in with a lockbox code or meet me there, and either works. I like meeting the agents I work with, and it helps to have you on site if the property needs a few finishing touches before I shoot.

What happens if the weather is bad?

Light rain or drizzle will not affect the photos. If conditions are severe, I will come back another day for the exterior shots. Weather can also affect whether drone photos can be done.

Can you add a twilight look to a listing?

Yes. I turn a daytime exterior into a warm evening-light image, which works well as the lead photo for the front of a home.

Which areas do you cover?

I photograph listings in Baltimore City, Baltimore County, Harford County, Carroll County, Howard County and parts of Anne Arundel County. If your listing sits just outside that area, ask.
